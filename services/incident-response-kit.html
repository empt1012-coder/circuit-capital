Incident Response Kit

What to do, in order, when something goes wrong and there is no security team to call: ransomware, a hacked mailbox, a lost laptop or a breach notice from a vendor. Checklists you can follow under pressure, plus the contact sheet and log you will wish you had filled in.

One-time price: $29Instant downloadPDF, Word, Markdown, Excel, CSV

Most small teams find out what their incident plan is during the incident. This kit is the plan, written down: who does what in the first hour, what to switch off and what to leave alone, who to call, and what to tell staff and customers. Every step is based on published guidance from CISA, the FBI’s IC3, the FTC, the UK’s NCSC, Microsoft, Google and Apple, and the sources are listed in the kit.

Buy the kit, $29

What’s inside

  • A 19-page PDF kit, ready to print:
    • How to use the kit: four things to do before you need it, four roles, three rules for every incident
    • The first 30 minutes: triage for when you don’t yet know what it is
    • Ransomware playbook: isolate, report, work out what was hit, recover, close out
    • Hacked email playbook for Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace: lock the attacker out, remove forwarding and inbox rules, check sign-ins and payments, give the account back
    • Lost or stolen laptop playbook: lock, cut access, decide whether to wipe, report
    • Vendor breach notice playbook: check the notice is real, work out your exposure, reduce risk, decide whether you have to tell anyone
    • Message templates: staff alert, customer warning after a hacked mailbox, questions to a breached vendor, a short holding line
    • Contact sheet and decision log, formatted to print
    • Sources: 17 linked pages from CISA, FBI IC3, U.S. Treasury, FTC, NCSC, NCSL, Microsoft, Google and Apple
  • Editable copies of every section in Word (.docx) and Markdown, so you can put in your own names, numbers and systems.
  • An incident workbook in Excel: decision log, affected-systems tracker, and the contact sheet. The log and contact sheet are also included as CSV.

Who it’s for

  • Owners, office managers and the one “IT person” at a company of roughly 3 to 50 people.
  • Teams on Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, with an outside IT provider or none at all.
  • Anyone whose cyber insurer, customer or auditor has asked whether you have an incident response plan. The kit gives you a written starting point; it does not certify anything.

Not sure yet? The free First 30 minutes after a security incident card is the first page of the kit, as a one-page printable PDF.

  1. Pay by card through Stripe’s secure checkout. Stripe emails you a receipt.

  3. If the download page doesn’t load or the file won’t open, email circuits50capital@circuits.fit with the email you paid with and we’ll send the kit directly.

What it is not

This is a practical checklist, not legal, insurance or forensic advice, and it does not replace an incident response firm when you need one. Several steps say to call your insurer, a lawyer or law enforcement, and those are the right calls. Product menus change; the kit links each vendor’s own instructions so you can check the current steps.
