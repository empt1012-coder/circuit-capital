Am I Affected? Check

When a security story is in the news — a breach at a vendor, a big outage, or a newly exploited product — tell us the story and the tools you use. Within 1 business day we email you, in plain English, whether you are affected and the 3 to 5 steps to take now.

Fixed price: $49One news storyDelivered in 1 business day

A headline can leave a small team wondering whether they have something to do, or nothing at all. This check is for that moment. You send us the story and a short list of the tools and vendors you use. We read the advisory or report against that list and write back with a straight answer: you are affected, you are not, or we cannot tell yet and what would settle it.

What you get back

  • A written answer, in plain English, of whether the story affects you given the tools and vendors you told us about.
  • A short list of 3 to 5 steps to take now, or a clear note that no action is needed.
  • Sources linked for the factual claims: vendor advisories, government catalogs or the original report. Where a source is unclear or out of date, we say so.
  • Delivery by email within 1 business day of when we have both your payment and the details below.

What to send us

  • The story: a link is best, or a short description if you only saw a headline.
  • The tools, vendors and products you use that might be related, with versions if you already know them.
  • Anything that changes the answer: company size, whether you have an outside IT provider, and whether the systems involved are yours or a vendor’s.

You do not need a complete inventory. A short list is enough to start. Checkout asks for the story and the tools or vendors you use; both fields are required. If you have more context after you pay, email it to us. If we need more, we will ask, and the 1 business day starts once you reply.

A sample answer

The text below is an example, based on a real catalog entry from November 2026. It is not an answer we sent to a customer, and the versions quoted are the ones published at the time.

The story. On November 10, 2026, CISA added Google Chromium CVE-2026-125350 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, with a federal due date of November 24. Google had described a use-after-free in Chromium’s XML component and released a Stable fix on November 9, with a further Stable build on November 11.

What you told us (example). You run Google Chrome on staff Windows and Mac laptops, you do not build your own Chromium software, and you are not sure which version is installed.

Are you affected. Chrome is in scope. You are only affected if a machine is still on a build older than the fixed Stable release. Google’s November 11 build was 157.0.8103.78 for Linux and 157.0.8103.78/.79 for Windows and Mac. Microsoft Edge and Opera can also be affected, because they use Chromium. CISA’s due date binds federal civilian agencies; for a private firm it is a priority signal, not a legal deadline.

What to do now.

  1. On a sample PC, open Chrome and go to chrome://settings/help (or Help > About Google Chrome). Confirm it shows 157.0.8103.78 or later.
  2. If you manage Chrome centrally, check the management console and force a relaunch so the update actually takes effect.
  3. Do the same for Microsoft Edge (edge://settings/help) and any other Chromium browser you use.
  4. Write down the versions you found, in case you need the note later.

Sources for this example: CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog; Google Chrome Releases, November 9 and November 11, 2026; our briefing on Chrome 157 and CVE-2026-125350.

What this is not

This is a research note, not incident response. If you think a system is already compromised, use the free First 30 minutes after a security incident card, and see the $29 Incident Response Kit. This check does not replace that work.

  • We do not log into your systems, run scans or test anything.
  • We do not contact the vendor for you, and we do not give legal, insurance or forensic advice.
  • One news story or advisory per check. A second story is a separate order.
  • Everything happens by email. There are no calls.

FAQ

What if you cannot tell whether we are affected?

If the public sources and what you sent are not enough, we tell you what is missing and what would settle it. If we cannot give a useful, sourced answer, we refund the fee.

What if the story is already a week or two old?

Email us first. If the check would no longer be useful, we will tell you before you pay. If you already paid, we will tell you within 1 business day and refund it.

Is the answer confidential?

Yes. We do not publish your question or our answer. If a story would make a good general briefing, we would ask first and remove anything identifying.
