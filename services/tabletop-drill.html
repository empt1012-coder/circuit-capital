Tabletop Drill

A 60 to 90 minute guided practice run of one security incident, for a small team, over a video call. We play out a realistic scenario step by step, your team talks through who does what, and within 2 business days you get a short written report of what worked, what was missing and what to fix first.

Fixed price: $99One scenario, up to 8 peopleReport in 2 business days

Most small teams have never practiced a bad day. The first time they work out who calls the bank, who talks to the insurer and where the backup login is kept is during a real incident, when every minute costs money. A tabletop drill is a calm rehearsal of that conversation, around a table or on a call, with no systems touched and no one graded.

Email to book, $99 How it works

What you get

  • A 60 to 90 minute guided session on a video call, run by Emilio Santos, for up to 8 people from your team.
  • One scenario, chosen with you from the list below and adjusted to the tools, vendors and IT provider you actually use.
  • A written after-action report within 2 business days: what went well, where the team got stuck, and up to 5 fixes in priority order, each with a suggested owner.
  • A one-page contact sheet template for the numbers you will want on a bad day: IT provider, insurer, bank, key vendors and who decides.
  • A dated record that your incident plan was tested, which you can keep with your insurance and client security paperwork.

Scenarios to choose from

  • Ransomware on a shared drive. Files will not open on Monday morning and a note asks for payment.
  • A hacked mailbox and a fake invoice. A customer says they paid your new bank account, which you never set up.
  • Your IT provider is breached. Your provider emails to say its remote-access tool was misused and some customer machines were reached.
  • A voice call approves a payment. Someone who sounds like the owner asks for an urgent transfer.
  • A lost laptop. A staff laptop with client files is left on a train.

How it works

  1. Book by email. Email circuits50capital@circuits.fit with the subject “Tabletop Drill”, the scenario you are interested in, your team size and two or three dates that suit you. We confirm an order within 1 business day.
  2. Short prep form. We send about 10 questions: who your IT provider and insurer are, how you take backups, how payments are approved, and who should attend. It takes about 15 minutes.
  3. The session. We set the scene, then reveal a new development every 10 to 15 minutes. The team says what it would do, who it would call and what it would need. We take notes and ask questions; we do not test or touch any system.
  4. The report. Within 2 business days of the session you get the after-action report and the contact sheet template by email.

After the date is confirmed by email, you’ll be sent a secure Stripe card payment link. The session is booked once payment is in.

Who it is for

Firms of roughly 5 to 50 people without a security team. The best group is the owner or manager who makes decisions, whoever handles finance and payments, the office manager, and your in-house IT person or a contact from your IT provider if they are willing to join.

What this is not

  • Not incident response. If you think something is wrong right now, use the free First 30 minutes after a security incident card and call your IT provider and insurer.
  • Not a technical test, audit or penetration test. We do not log in to your systems or scan anything.
  • Not legal or insurance advice. For notification duties and coverage questions, ask your lawyer and your broker.

If you want written playbooks to drill against, the $29 Incident Response Kit has step-by-step plans for ransomware, a hacked mailbox, a lost laptop and a vendor breach notice. If you would rather run a drill yourself, CISA publishes free tabletop exercise packages.

Your data

  • We do not record the session unless you ask us to.
  • Your prep form, our notes and the report are kept in the circuits50capital@circuits.fit mailbox. Notes are drafted with AI assistance and reviewed by Emilio Santos before the report is sent.
  • We delete the prep form and working notes after the report is delivered. You keep the report. See the privacy policy.

FAQ

Do we need a written incident plan first?

No. Many teams find the drill is the fastest way to write one. The report lists what your plan should say.

Can we reschedule?

Yes, once, at no cost, with at least one business day’s notice. If we have to cancel and cannot offer a date that works for you, we refund the fee.

Can our IT provider join?

Yes, and it usually helps. The drill is a good way to see how your provider would actually respond and what it needs from you.
