Security and vulnerabilities

Every Circuit & Capital briefing, guide and free tool on security and vulnerabilities: CISA KEV roundups, vendor patch questions, SBOMs, risk exceptions, cyber insurance and SEC disclosure.

Security news arrives as a stream of CVE numbers. Most of them do not matter to a given company, and a few matter a great deal. The useful filter is CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog: a bug only goes on it when CISA has evidence that attackers are using it. Each entry carries a due date that binds federal civilian agencies under Binding Operational Directive 26-04. Private companies are not bound by those dates, but boards, auditors and insurers increasingly treat them as the benchmark for a reasonable response.

Circuit & Capital covers catalog additions that reach ordinary businesses, and writes them for the people who sign off on the response: the CFO who owns the contracts and the CISO who owns the controls. Each briefing gives the dates, the affected versions confirmed against the vendor’s advisory, and the questions to put to whoever runs the system. Increasingly that is not your own team. Storefronts are run by agencies, network consoles by managed providers, and libraries like PHP or Brotli ship inside products you bought. The work is often a vendor inventory and a contract question, not only a patch.

The guides on this page cover the controls that sit around those bugs. How to ask a vendor for a software bill of materials that meets CISA’s 2026 minimum elements. How to keep a risk exception register where every gap has an owner, a funded fix and an expiry date, and where known-exploited bugs, MFA, backups and EDR are never waived. What a cyber insurer will still ask at renewal. And how to prepare for the SEC’s four-business-day Form 8-K clock, which starts when the company decides an incident is material.

The free tools turn those guides into worksheets that run in the browser and keep your data on your device. Each now has a free download, a CSV, spreadsheet, PDF or checklist, so you can keep a copy in the place your auditors expect.

Everything here follows the site’s editorial standards: every fact is checked against a primary source such as the CISA catalog, the vendor advisory or NVD, and anything we cannot confirm is left out. Corrections are logged publicly.
