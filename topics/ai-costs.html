AI costs

Guides, free calculators and briefings on what AI actually costs: token prices, cost per finished task, review time, gross margin, self-hosting break-even and agent access limits.

The price on an AI model’s pricing page is the cost of one attempt. It is not the cost of the work. A cheaper model that fails more often, needs retries or sends more output to a human reviewer can cost more per finished task than a model with a higher list price. Most AI business cases skip that step, which is why so many show savings on paper and a larger bill in practice.

This page brings together Circuit & Capital’s coverage of AI costs for the people who approve the spend. The guides explain how to tell AI spend from AI return, how to put a cost on one workflow before scaling it, and how to write a one-page rule for who may use which tools. The briefings look at the market around that spend: Gartner’s forecasts for AI spending and infrastructure impairment, vendor speed claims and whose number they are, and the unit economics of companies built on models they rent.

Two free calculators do the arithmetic. AI model cost per finished task compares models on attempt cost, retries, success rate and review time, and finds the daily token volume at which self-hosting costs less than an API. Token cost to gross margin starts from what you charge per task and shows how much token and review cost leave as contribution. Both now come with a free spreadsheet download with the formulas visible, so finance can check the logic and plug in its own numbers.

Agents add a second kind of cost: what they can touch. An agent that inherits a person’s access, has no spend cap and no named owner is a liability before it is a saving. The agent inventory and agent access limit sheet record what each agent may read, write, call and spend, and who answers for it. Regulators have noticed. The SEC’s fiscal 2027 examination priorities say examiners will review how registrants monitor the AI tools they use.

The rule across all of it is simple. Use your own volumes and prices. Treat a vendor benchmark as the vendor’s claim until you have run the test yourself. And count human review time, because it is often the largest line.
