Notion AI chat transcripts were exposed at a vendor. Keep passwords out of the chat.

Notion’s advisory, first posted November 10, says a security incident at Fireworks AI, the vendor Notion used for its Insights feature and for some open-weight models, affected fewer than 1% of workspaces. The exposed data is Notion AI chat transcripts used for Insights, and Notion says that for the vast majority of impacted workspaces the chats were not labeled with a user or a workspace. Notion has turned Insights off, stopped using Fireworks, and on November 11 revoked exposed Notion API keys it found in those messages. If Notion has not contacted you, it says no action is needed right now.

Edited by Emilio Santos November 12, 2026 5 min read

On November 10, 2026, Notion published an advisory about a security incident at Fireworks AI, a company it used to run classification jobs for a feature called Insights and to serve some open-weight models. Notion says the incident affects fewer than 1% of workspaces. The data involved is Notion AI chat transcripts used for Insights. Notion says that for the vast majority of impacted workspaces those chats were “unattributable”: they were not labeled with who wrote them or which workspace they came from, and they did not contain tool-call results.

The practical point is wider than one product. Anything you type into an AI chat can end up with that tool’s own vendors. Keep passwords and API keys out of chat windows, share them through a password manager, and change any you have pasted. A short one-page AI use rule can make that a team habit rather than a reminder you send after the fact.

What happened

On the evening of November 2, Fireworks notified Notion that a small set of data had been accessed by malicious actors. Notion says that dataset included Notion AI synthetic testing data, and that no Notion AI customer data was involved at that stage. By the morning of November 3, Notion says it had disconnected its systems from Fireworks, deleted all data in the Fireworks system, and stopped all communication with it.

On November 6, Fireworks notified Notion that malicious actors may have accessed potential customer data. Notion began another investigation. On November 8, Fireworks confirmed the final impacted dataset. On November 9, Notion reached out to impacted customers.

After reviewing the November 8 dataset, Notion says it included Notion AI chat transcripts used for the Insights feature. For the vast majority of impacted workspaces, Notion says, those chats were unattributable: they were not labeled with a user or a workspace, and they did not contain tool-call results. Notion says no other Notion features were affected, and that this was not a breach of Notion’s own systems.

The Insights feature has been disabled while Notion investigates, and Notion is no longer serving open-weight models through Fireworks. On November 11, Notion added an update: it had revoked exposed Notion API keys that were found in affected user messages during its investigation. The advisory was first published November 10. Notion also made a style and clarity update that same day, and said no previously reported facts had changed.

Who is affected, and what to do

Notion says it has contacted customers whose workspaces were affected. If you have not heard from Notion, it says it has not identified your workspace as affected, and no action is needed right now. The investigation is ongoing, and Notion says it will contact you directly if that changes.

If Notion has contacted you, follow the instructions in that email. Notion says affected customers can retrieve and review the impacted data securely inside their workspace, and it recommends treating that data as if it were exposed. If anyone in an affected workspace pasted sensitive information, passwords, API keys or other credentials into Notion AI during the timeframe of the incident, rotate them.

Going forward, treat an AI chat window the same way you would treat an email to a vendor: useful for work, not a place for secrets. Share logins through a password manager, not by pasting them into a prompt.
