Gartner revised the AI bill. Most of it is not your budget.

The September forecast is $2.7 trillion, up 49.5%. About 56% of the table is infrastructure. The line a company actually signs is the feature stuffed into software it already owns.

By Emilio Santos October 1, 2026 8 min read

On September 16, Gartner revised its 2026 AI spending forecast to $2.7 trillion, a 49.5% increase from 2025. In May the same house had said $2.59 trillion and 47%. The headline moved. The buyer did not. John-David Lovelock, a distinguished vice president analyst at Gartner, said demand for AI infrastructure is still strong and inelastic to higher memory prices, and that hyperscalers and service providers buying AI-optimized servers remain the largest single area of spending.

Read the table under the headline before you take the round number into a board pack. Gartner’s line items for 2026 sum to $2.67 trillion. AI infrastructure is $1.48 trillion of that. AI services are $576 billion. AI software is $462 billion. AI cybersecurity is $51 billion, almost double the 2025 line. Agents and assistants are $29 billion. Generative AI models are $28 billion. Infrastructure is about 56% of the table. The software a mid-market company can actually put on a purchase order is a minority of the story Gartner is telling.

What changed since May

Gartner raised two near-term growth rates in this quarter’s forecast. Spending on AI application-development platforms is now expected to grow 39% in 2026, up from 28% in the prior forecast, because enterprises, software vendors, and services firms want custom applications. Growth in generative AI models moved from 110% to 117%. Gartner called that a small but growing opening for domain-specific models that cost less and fit a job, not a general demo.

It also split cross-functional agents and assistants out of the AI software line, and added consumer agents, so the new table is not a clean restatement of the May pile. Do not subtract $2.59 trillion from $2.7 trillion and call the difference new enterprise demand. Part of the revision is a bigger infrastructure build. Part of it is a new way of counting agents.

If more than half the forecast is servers, chips, networks, and AI cloud, matching “the industry” is not a budget. It is a category error.

Where the company’s check actually lands

Gartner says generative AI is in the trough of disillusionment in 2026, and that enterprises are using simpler AI features embedded in software they already have: faster workflows, customer responses, decisions. Lovelock’s other point is the one a CFO can use. Buyers are going to service firms less often for a transformation program and more often for smaller projects that turn on the AI already inside an incumbent system. Gartner still sees those two kinds of work combining into a $1.2 trillion AI-services opportunity by 2030. That is a vendor forecast. It is also a description of the renewal you will see this quarter: the suite you own, plus an agent SKU, plus a services line to switch it on.

That is a different problem from the $2.7 trillion. You do not buy the hyperscaler’s data center. You do buy the seat, the consumption overage, and the implementation that “just enables” the feature the vendor shipped to stay relevant. Gartner said vendors are embedding agentic AI in existing products to defend against new cross-functional agents. The defense shows up on your invoice.

What to take to the next renewal

Separate three numbers and refuse to let them share a slide. One: industry infrastructure spend, which you do not control. Two: the AI line inside a product you already license. Three: a workflow with a definition of done, and whether that workflow moved.

Ask the vendor which of Gartner’s buckets the quote sits in. An embedded feature in a suite you renew is software. A usage meter on a model is not a seat. A partner “to turn it on” is services. If the quote will not say which, you are being sold the headline.

The May piece on this site still stands on the part that was never about the size of the forecast. A larger industry bill does not create a return on your line. Gartner revised the bill upward in four months. The question for an operator is unchanged: which check on this forecast is yours, and what does it retire?
