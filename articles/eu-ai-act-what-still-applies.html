The EU AI Act did not slip. The high-risk part did.

Article 50 transparency has been in force since August 2. Stand-alone high-risk duties move to December 2, 2027. A US buyer still has to ask which date the vendor is selling.

By Emilio Santos October 1, 2026 8 min read

The Council of the European Union’s timeline for the Artificial Intelligence Act, updated after its June 29, 2026 sign-off, splits the law into dates a buyer can actually use. Most of the Act’s rules reached their general application on August 2, 2026. The high-risk regime did not. The Council records a fixed delay: December 2, 2027 for stand-alone high-risk systems, and August 2, 2028 for high-risk systems embedded in products. The European Commission’s AI Act Service Desk, which says its timeline includes the Digital Omnibus amendments, lists the same two high-risk dates, and a nearer one: December 2, 2026.

This briefing is for a company that buys software, including a company outside the EU whose vendor, customer, or data sits inside it. It is not a determination that your system is high-risk. That is a legal question. The operator question is whether the contract names a date.

What is already on

The Service Desk says transparency rules in Article 50 started to apply on August 2, 2026, along with enforcement for those rules, for prohibitions already in force, for general-purpose AI, and for AI literacy. On December 2, 2026 two further things arrive. New prohibitions cover AI systems that generate non-consensual sexual deepfakes and child sexual abuse material. And providers of certain generative systems that were already on the market before August 2, 2026 hit the end of the transition for the Article 50(2) machine-readable marking duty.

So “the AI Act was delayed” is the wrong sentence in a renewal. The delay the Council adopted is the high-risk application date. Transparency, and the December marking deadline for systems already shipping, were not given that same extra year.

What a vendor will try to collapse

A pitch that says “high-risk is 2027, so we are fine” may be describing Annex III stand-alone systems, the employment, education, credit, and essential-service uses the delay covers. It may also be describing a chatbot, a synthetic image, or a feature inside a suite you already license. Those are not the same article of the regulation. Ask the vendor to name the article, the annex, and the date, in the order form or in a schedule. If they cannot, you do not have a compliance claim. You have a slogan.

A delay of the high-risk chapter is not a delay of the duty to say that a person is talking to a machine.

What to write down this month

List every AI feature you have turned on, or that a vendor turned on inside a product, that a customer or employee in the EU can reach. Next to each one, write whether the output is synthetic, whether a person is told, and whether the vendor’s marking deadline is December 2, 2026 or a later high-risk date. Keep the Council timeline and the Service Desk timeline in the packet. When they disagree with a salesperson, the public timeline wins until counsel says otherwise.
